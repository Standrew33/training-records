# Training records – short report

**What I built and why.** A C# / .NET 10 solution in four layers: `Skoleni.Core` (domain: obligations, validity status, planner; no dependencies), `Skoleni.Data` (reading and cleaning the four exports), `Skoleni.DataPrep` (a one-off step: raw CSV → cleaned `dataset.json`) and `Skoleni.Web` (Blazor WebAssembly, presentation only). The site is static and runs free on GitHub Pages – no server, no accounts. Raw data never reaches it: cleaning happens beforehand and the output contains no national ID numbers or free-text notes (the model has no field for them; a test guards that). Screens: Overview (what needs attention, status by training and department), People (filter by department / manager, CSV export), Sign-up plan, Terms, and Data quality, where every data change states what was wrong and what I did about it – nothing is corrected silently.

**What I found in the data.** The exports are deliberately dirty (23 errors, 66 warnings, 111 minor items). Most important: `absolvovana_skoleni.csv` is Windows-1250 and semicolon-separated; five date formats including Excel serial numbers and a 1900 placeholder; an export comment ("do not count"), a header in the middle and a "Celkem" total row; 15 repeated record IDs; wrong course codes (`KYB3R` is fixed unambiguously, `BOZP-X` and `GDPR-2` are not – that would be a guess, so those records are dropped and reported); 4 duplicate people (same national ID number, different employee ID) and 9 records for non-existent IDs; departments spelled in several ways or missing; validity written as "2 roky"; in terms an impossible date (31 Sep), a duplicate term, zero capacity, overbooked and cancelled terms, a weekend term and prices such as "2 100 Kč".
**Assumptions (decisions, not facts):** validity is computed from the catalogue, not from the `platnost_do` column (it disagrees for 34 records; I do not know which is right); `0 months` = never expires; parental leave and terminated employees are neither counted nor planned; a new hire / person with no record has 30 days from the start date; "expiring soon" = 90 days (switchable); "today" = 1 Oct 2026 (changeable); records dated after the export ("zadáno předem") do not count as completed.

**How I plan, and where I am unsure.** The planner assigns people already past the deadline first, then by expiry date, each to the nearest term with a free seat (capacity − booked), without collisions and not after employment ends. My trust in it comes from tests: invariants on the real data and a property test against brute force (it finds an on-time assignment whenever one exists). Result on the data: 201 sign-ups (about CZK 162k), but **55 people's validity runs out before a term can take them** (43 of them cybersecurity); 14 of those are down to the calendar, 41 to full earlier terms – so the sensible recommendation is "schedule an earlier term", not a bug. Putting already-expired people first may raise that number; I did not compare policies. The same figures (751 obligations, 77 problems, 201 sign-ups) came out of my earlier independent prototype in another language, which shows two implementations agree, not that the logic is right.

**What I did not do / left out deliberately.** The app does not know *who* holds the "booked" seats – it may propose someone already registered. Sign-ups are not stored or sent (no backend, e-mail or manual plan editing). Optional trainings (ANGL) are not evaluated. Data is loaded by the build, not uploaded in the browser. No UI tests; accessibility is basic. The planner is greedy – no global optimum; collisions between different courses are handled by a heuristic only.

**What next.** Confirm assumptions with HR (grace period for new hires, the source of `platnost_do`, parental leave); a system of record for sign-ups (who is on a term) and notifications to managers; uploading an export in the app; comparing planning policies and adding manual plan edits; fixing the data errors at the source so cleaning need not repeat.
